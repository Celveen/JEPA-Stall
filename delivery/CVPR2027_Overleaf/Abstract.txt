World-model planners select actions by minimizing a predicted distance to the goal, but this objective is blind to what the controller has already tried. Latent planners therefore stall: they keep acting while revisiting the same configurations, even when the goal is nearly reached. We propose Execution-Conditioned Planning (ECP), which turns an episode's own execution history into a corrective planning signal. ECP detects stagnation from realized latent movement, stores stalled observations in an episode-local memory that repels predicted trajectories from revisiting them, and temporarily reweights the goal toward its unfinished proprioceptive component. Inspired by DART and ACT, we combine noisy, interaction-biased data collection for training with sustained planning corrections during inference. ECP adds no learned parameters, acts entirely through the existing action optimizer, and applies to world models with fixed parameters or online adaptation. On PushObj, success rises from 43.7% to 72.3% with a fixed pretrained ResNet world model and from 69.4% to 77.7% with AdaJEPA online updates. On PushT, we achieve 86% clean success and 63.7% mean success across six visual shifts. ECP improves Maze success without regressions and reduces Chamfer distance by 31.7% on Rope with DINO-WM.
