\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\input{tables/world_model_comparison}
We test whether execution feedback improves task completion, how data augmentation and planning correction interact, and which mechanisms account for the gains.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Tasks and metrics.}
PushObj comprises 350 image-goal tasks across seven object shapes; I, small-T, and square are unseen during training. Maze comprises 100 navigation tasks across five held-out layouts. PushT evaluates 50 matched start--goal tasks under clean observations and six visual shifts: blur, salt-and-pepper noise, darker lighting, and changes to the agent, object, or goal color. Manipulation success requires a joint pusher/object position error below 20 pixels and an object-angle error below $\pi/9$; Maze uses a 0.5-unit position threshold.

\paragraph{Methods and planning budget.}
We compare with DINO-WM~\cite{zhou2025dinowm}, LeWM~\cite{maes2026lewm}, DDP-WM~\cite{yin2026ddpwm}, and the released world model with frozen parameters or AdaJEPA updates~\cite{wang2026adajepa}. All methods receive the same 150-control execution budget and use their own gradient-descent (GD) or cross-entropy (CEM) planner. Our evaluations allow 30 MPC replans, each executing five controls from a 25-control horizon; Maze uses action repeat four. Every ECP-on/off comparison is paired: both runs share the checkpoint, tasks, and action initialization, so each difference is attributable to the correction. ECP denotes $S{+}P$ for manipulation and $S$ for Maze. Planner schedules, checkpoint provenance, and task construction are specified in \cref{sec:implementation,sec:pusht-extension}.

\subsection{Comparison with recent world models}
\Cref{tab:main} compares our approach with recent world-model controllers. Our AdaJEPA variant attains the best result in 13 of 16 columns, including both averages: 77.7\% on PushObj and 63.7\% across the six PushT visual shifts, exceeding the strongest baseline by 8.3 and 7.4 points, respectively. Our frozen variant reaches 72.3\% on PushObj and thereby surpasses released AdaJEPA (69.4\%) without any test-time gradient update. On PushT, frozen ECP raises clean success from 72\% to 82\% and the shift mean from 46.0\% to 55.7\%, and ECP never lowers success in any of the 28 checkpoint--condition pairs (\cref{tab:pusht-ecp}).

\paragraph{Transfer to LeWM.}
Execution-conditioned correction also extends to a different backbone. On a frozen augmented LeWM, ECP solves 231 of 350 tasks (66.0\%) versus 215 (61.4\%) for native planning (\cref{sec:backbone}).

\subsection{Manipulation and navigation}
\paragraph{ECP improves every configuration.}
ECP raises PushObj success for both checkpoints and both update rules (\cref{tab:components,tab:threshold}). With the released checkpoint, where no training is involved, ECP alone raises frozen success from 43.7\% to 59.7\% and adaptive success from 69.4\% to 73.1\%. With the augmented checkpoint, it raises frozen success from 61.1\% to 72.3\% and adaptive success from 74.9\% to 77.7\%. Together, augmentation and ECP gain 28.6 and 8.3 points over the released native baselines (\cref{fig:headline}).

\paragraph{Recovering navigation with stagnation memory.}
On Maze, movement-triggered memory raises frozen success from 48\% to 50\%, and gated memory raises AdaJEPA success from 63\% to 66\% (\cref{tab:maze}). Neither changes a single native success into a failure. Both use the released checkpoint without new navigation training, so the regularizer transfers directly from manipulation to navigation.
