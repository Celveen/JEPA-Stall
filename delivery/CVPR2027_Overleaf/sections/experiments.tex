\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\input{tables/world_model_comparison}
We evaluate ECP on four task families and three world-model backbones, examining its performance against recent controllers, its generality, and the source of its gains.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Tasks and metrics.}
All tasks are specified by a goal image: \emph{PushObj} comprises 350 pushing tasks over seven object shapes, of which I, small-T, and square are unseen during training. \emph{PushT} uses 50 matched start--goal pairs under clean observations and six visual shifts: blur, salt-and-pepper noise, darker lighting, and a recolored agent, object, or goal. \emph{Maze} comprises 100 navigation tasks across five held-out layouts, and \emph{Rope} 20 deformable-object targets repeated under three seeds. A PushObj or PushT episode succeeds when the joint pusher/object position error is below 20 pixels and the object-angle error below $\pi/9$; a Maze episode succeeds when the position error is below 0.5 units. On Rope we report the final Chamfer distance (CD) and count success at CD below 0.3, with at most 20 pushes.

\paragraph{World models and baselines.}
Our primary world model couples a ResNet visual encoder with a latent dynamics predictor and is pretrained offline. \emph{Frozen} keeps all its parameters fixed during execution, whereas \emph{AdaJEPA} updates them from executed transitions~\cite{wang2026adajepa}. \emph{Released} denotes the published checkpoint and \emph{Data aug.} a checkpoint trained offline on our interaction-biased data. \emph{Native} is the original planner without ECP. We compare with DINO-WM~\cite{zhou2025dinowm} and LeWM~\cite{maes2026lewm}, and also apply ECP to both with their parameters fixed. \emph{Ours} combines augmented training with ECP on PushObj and PushT, and adds ECP to the released models on Maze and Rope.

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
PushObj and PushT controllers receive 150 control calls and retain their gradient-descent (GD) or cross-entropy (CEM) planner. The ResNet controllers allow 30 replans, each executing five controls from a 25-control horizon; Maze repeats each control four times. Rope keeps DINO-WM fixed, predicts and executes three pushes before replanning, and observes after every push. Across tasks, the ECP detection window spans $W=3$ feedback steps, where a feedback step is one completed replan on PushObj, PushT, and Maze and one push on Rope, and detection starts after a task-specific warm-up. The thresholds satisfy $\gamma_{\rm lo}=\gamma_{\rm hi}/3$, with $\gamma_{\rm hi}=0.45$ for the main PushObj results and $0.15$ for PushT, Maze, Rope, and the PushObj component studies. The memory uses $\beta=0.25$ and $\kappa=2$, and the goal weight is multiplied by $b=10$ for three planning calls. Writing $S$ for stagnation memory and $P$ for proprioceptive reweighting, ECP uses $S{+}P$ on PushObj and PushT and memory-only $S$ on Maze and Rope. On Rope, each detected memory has unit weight, giving fixed strength $\kappa=2$, after five warm-up pushes. Each comparison with and without ECP shares the checkpoint, tasks, initialization, and execution budget, with ECP as the intended configuration difference. \Cref{tab:parameters} lists the remaining settings, including those of the loss gate and of data collection.

\Cref{tab:main} compares complete controllers under a common execution budget. Our AdaJEPA variant obtains the highest average success on both benchmarks, exceeding the strongest baseline by 8.3 points on PushObj and by 7.4 points across the six PushT visual shifts, and it attains the best result in 13 of the 16 columns. The fixed-parameter variant is equally informative: without a single online model update, it outperforms the released AdaJEPA controller on PushObj, which adapts its model after every replan. Fixed-weight controls built on R3M, DINO CLS, and DDP-WM also remain below both of our controllers on average (\cref{tab:extra-baselines}).

\subsection{Generality across backbones, tasks, and updates}
\label{sec:generality}
\paragraph{Other backbones.}
ECP is not tied to the ResNet world model. On LeWM with fixed parameters, augmentation and ECP together gain 18.6 points on PushObj over matched random-data training (\cref{fig:headline}). Here memory and pusher reweighting alone change native success by less than one point, and the full gain is obtained with the LeWM implementation of ECP, which adds a contact-directed term to the corrected objective (\cref{sec:backbone,tab:lewm-contact}).

\paragraph{Checkpoints and update rules.}
On PushObj, ECP improves the ResNet world model whether its weights are released or trained on augmented data, and whether they are fixed or adapted online (\cref{tab:components,fig:threshold}). The released checkpoint isolates the effect of planning: with no additional training, ECP gains 16.0 points with fixed parameters and 3.7 points with AdaJEPA.

\paragraph{Navigation and deformable objects.}
Stagnation memory also transfers beyond manipulation. On Maze, with the released checkpoint and no new offline training, it raises success from 48\% to 50\% with fixed parameters and from 63\% to 66\% with AdaJEPA under the loss gate, without losing a single native success (\cref{tab:maze}). Rope changes the dynamics, the backbone, and the optimizer at once. Across three seeds with the same three-push horizon, DINO-WM (ours) lowers mean final CD from 0.6865 to 0.4793 (30.2\%) and raises success from 55.0\% to 71.7\%. Mean executed pushes fall from 12.68 to 11.30 (10.9\%), including unsuccessful runs (\cref{tab:rope}). CD and push count improve in every seed; strength and warm-up ablations appear in \cref{sec:rope-details}.
