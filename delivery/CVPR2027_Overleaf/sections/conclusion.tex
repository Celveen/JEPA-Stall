\section{Conclusion}
We studied a failure mode shared by latent world-model planners: because their objective carries no memory of execution, they stall, repeating plans that have already failed. Execution-Conditioned Planning addresses this failure with information that the episode itself provides, by detecting stagnation, remembering where execution stalled, and temporarily emphasizing the goal component left unfinished. It requires no learned parameters, works with fixed or online-adapted world models, and, together with interaction-biased training, improves manipulation, navigation, and deformable-object planning across ResNet, LeWM, and DINO-WM controllers. More broadly, our results suggest that an episode's own history is an underused resource for planning with learned world models. Future work includes shift-robust stall detection and learned recovery behaviors (\cref{sec:pending}).
