\begin{abstract}
Latent world models let agents plan toward an image goal, but repeated replanning can settle into local cycles even when the controller keeps acting. Recent execution reveals where planning has failed, yet a goal-distance objective does not remember those failures. We introduce execution-conditioned planning (ECP) to use this feedback in the next plan. ECP stores observations at which progress stalls and adds a local penalty against returning to them. Temporary proprioceptive reweighting helps finish the agent-position component of the task. Both corrections act on action optimization, so they work with frozen models or continued test-time adaptation. An interaction-biased data collection recipe complements ECP by supplying contact-rich transitions during offline training. Paired manipulation and navigation evaluations test these contributions under fixed execution budgets. On PushObj, the combined approach raises success from 43.7\% to 72.3\% with frozen dynamics and from 69.4\% to 76.9\% with AdaJEPA, using 30 MPC replans. On PushT, it outperforms the evaluated state-of-the-art world-model controllers, reaching 63.7\% mean success across six visual shifts.
\end{abstract}
