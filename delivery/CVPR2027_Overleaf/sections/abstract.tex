\begin{abstract}
World-model planners select actions by minimizing a predicted distance to the goal, but this objective is blind to what the controller has already tried. Latent planners therefore stall: they keep acting while revisiting the same configurations, even when the goal is nearly reached. We introduce Execution-Conditioned Planning (ECP), which turns an episode's own execution history into a corrective planning signal. ECP detects stagnation from realized latent movement, stores stalled observations in an episode-local memory that repels predicted trajectories from revisiting them, and temporarily reweights the goal toward its unfinished proprioceptive component. We pair ECP with interaction-biased data augmentation that supplies contact-rich transitions for learning dynamics. Our training and inference design is inspired by DART's noise-injected data collection and ACT's temporally extended action chunks, respectively. ECP adds no learned parameters, acts entirely through the existing action optimizer, and applies to world models with fixed parameters or online adaptation. On PushObj, our approach raises offline-pretrained ResNet planning without online updates from 43.7\% to 72.3\% and online-adapting AdaJEPA from 69.4\% to 77.7\%, and the gains hold across a fourfold range of the trigger threshold. On PushT, it attains 86\% clean success and 63.7\% mean success across six visual shifts, the best among all compared world-model controllers. ECP also improves Maze navigation without regressions and reduces Rope Chamfer distance by 31.7\%.
\end{abstract}
