Latent world models can plan directly from image goals, yet their objective remains Markovian and memoryless: it measures only the predicted distance to the goal and keeps no record of what the controller has already tried. When model mismatch, non-convex latent geometry, or multi-branch scale asymmetries prevent a plan from making progress, replanning from nearly the same state minimizes the same cost and reproduces the failed actions—trapping the active controller in a closed-loop limit cycle we call a stall. We propose Execution-Conditioned Planning (ECP), a framework that turns an episode's own execution history into a corrective planning signal. ECP detects stalls by comparing recent latent movement with remaining goal distance. It remembers stalled states to discourage revisits without penalizing the goal, and temporarily increases the weight of unfinished proprioceptive goal components. Inspired by DART and ACT, we pair noisy, interaction-biased training data with sustained planning corrections at inference. ECP adds no learned parameters, acts entirely through the existing action optimizer, and applies to both frozen and online-adapted world models. On PushObj, success rises from 43.7% to 72.3% (+28.6 pp) with a frozen ResNet world model—surpassing online-adapting AdaJEPA (69.4%)—and reaches 77.7% with AdaJEPA. On PushT, we achieve 86.0% clean and 63.7% mean success across six visual shifts. ECP improves Maze navigation without regressions and reduces Rope Chamfer distance by 30.2% with DINO-WM across three seeds. Code will be provided in the supplementary material.
