\paragraph{Training data and planning correction are complementary.}
Offline training coverage and online planning correction act at different stages, and their gains accumulate: augmentation improves the ResNet world model on PushObj by +17.4~pp (frozen) and +5.4~pp (AdaJEPA), while ECP adds a further +11.1~pp and +2.9~pp on the same checkpoints (\cref{fig:headline}). This training benefit transfers across backbones: under matched training, it raises frozen DINO-WM with CEM from 24.6\% to 48.9\% across all seven shapes (\cref{tab:dinowm-aug}), far exceeding variation across training seeds (\cref{tab:training}).

\paragraph{Both corrections contribute, in complementary roles.}
Each correction alone improves over native planning across both checkpoints and update rules (\cref{tab:components}). On PushObj, where stalls occur once the object is nearly aligned while the pusher position remains open, temporary reweighting ($P$) drives most of the gain. On Maze with AdaJEPA, where stalls arise from corridor oscillation rather than subspace imbalance, the roles reverse: stagnation memory ($S$) alone attains the highest success (66.0\%) without regressions (\cref{tab:maze-controls}). Memory is most effective on the \emph{joint} visual--proprioceptive latent, which separates scenes sharing an agent position and outperforms proprioception-only memory at every tested strength (\cref{tab:memory-strength}). An action-space variant applying a bounded pusher-goal offset upon physical stagnation similarly improves AdaJEPA across two task streams (\cref{tab:action}).

\paragraph{ECP is robust to the trigger threshold.}
\Cref{fig:threshold} varies $\gamma_{\rm hi}$ over a fourfold range. Larger thresholds trigger earlier and in more episodes, yet success varies by less than one percentage point (76.9\%--77.7\%) and always above native planning, with at most two regressions. Gains appear on seen and unseen shapes alike with fixed weights and concentrate on seen shapes with AdaJEPA (\cref{tab:threshold-shape}).

\paragraph{Conditional correction outperforms permanent reweighting and deeper search.}
Applying the $10\times$ multiplier permanently rescues 50 failures but \textbf{breaks 32 episodes that native MPC solves} (50~/~32), for less than half the net gain of ECP (\cref{tab:timing}). Triggered ECP preserves every native success while recovering \textbf{39 failures (39~/~0)}, modifying the cost only where native planning has stalled. Deeper search does not resolve stalls either: a lookahead planner evaluating candidate prefixes improves net success only marginally while introducing \textbf{27 regressions} (34~/~27), and adding lookahead to ECP is worse than ECP alone (\cref{sec:additional-controls}). These controls support correcting the objective rather than only increasing search.

\paragraph{Loss-gated coordination complements online adaptation.}
When the model adapts online on Maze, intervention timing matters: correcting at the first stall recovers failures but loses 4 native successes (7~/~4), and a fixed delay is worse than not correcting (\cref{tab:maze-controls}). Freezing adaptation at the stall point without correcting the objective collapses 50-replan success from 82\% to 60\% (\cref{tab:maze-full}). The loss gate (\cref{eq:gate}) avoids both pitfalls, retaining every native success at 30 replans (3~/~0) and reaching 87\% at 50 replans.

\subsection{Qualitative and behavioral recovery}
\label{sec:qualitative}
In the PushObj episode of \cref{fig:teaser}a, the native frozen controller brings the object nearly to its goal by replan 15 and then circles locally without closing the pusher error; ECP shares the first 15 plans, detects the stall, and completes the task two replans later (\cref{fig:push-distance}). Across triggered episodes, ECP moves the pusher $>15$~px from the stalled position within three replans \textbf{38\% of the time vs.\ 11\% natively}, and reduces object error or succeeds within ten replans in \textbf{34\% vs.\ 1\% natively} (\cref{sec:behavior}). In Maze, stagnation memory makes dead-end corridors costly and redirects the trajectory (\cref{fig:teaser}b,d), while PushT and Rope rollouts show intermediate frames of further recoveries (\cref{fig:pusht-rollouts,fig:rope-rollouts}).
