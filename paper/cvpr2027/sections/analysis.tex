\subsection{Ablation studies}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Training data and planning correction are complementary.}
Training and planning act at different stages, and their gains accumulate: augmentation improves the ResNet world model on PushObj by 17.4 points with fixed parameters and by 5.4 points with AdaJEPA, and ECP adds a further 11.1 and 2.9 points on the same checkpoints (\cref{fig:headline}). The benefit of augmentation is not specific to our backbone: under matched training, it raises DINO-WM with CEM from 24.6\% to 48.9\% and improves all seven shapes (\cref{tab:dinowm-aug}). Both augmentation gains exceed the variation of native success across three training initializations (\cref{tab:training}).

\paragraph{Both corrections contribute, in different tasks.}
Each correction improves native planning across checkpoints and update rules (\cref{tab:components}). On PushObj, goal reweighting accounts for most of the gain; adding memory changes at most one outcome, consistent with stalls dominated by unfinished pusher alignment. On Maze with AdaJEPA, memory alone yields the highest success without regressions (\cref{tab:maze-controls}). Joint visual/proprioceptive memory outperforms proprioception-only memory at every tested strength, separating scenes that share an agent position (\cref{tab:memory-strength}). The components thus address unfinished goals and revisited regions. An action-space variant using physical displacement and a bounded pusher-goal offset also improves AdaJEPA on two task streams (\cref{tab:action}).

\paragraph{ECP is robust to the trigger threshold.}
\Cref{fig:threshold} varies the threshold below which movement counts as a stall over a fourfold range. Larger thresholds trigger in more episodes and earlier, yet success stays within one point across the range and always above native planning, with at most two regressions. Per shape, gains appear on seen and unseen shapes alike with fixed parameters and concentrate on seen shapes with AdaJEPA (\cref{tab:threshold-shape}).

\paragraph{Conditional correction outperforms permanent reweighting and longer search.}
Permanent reweighting breaks 32 native successes and yields less than half ECP's net gain (\cref{tab:timing}). Triggered correction preserves every native success while recovering 39 failures. Lookahead search adds only a marginal gain and introduces 27 regressions; combining it with ECP performs worse than ECP alone (\cref{sec:additional-controls}). Optimizing an objective more thoroughly does not resolve its preference for an unproductive plan.

\paragraph{Correction complements continued adaptation.}
When the model is still learning, the schedule of correction matters: on Maze with AdaJEPA, correcting at the first detected stall recovers the most failures but also loses native successes, and delaying correction by a fixed interval is worse than not correcting at all. The loss gate avoids both problems: gated memory retains every native success while solving three additional tasks (\cref{tab:maze-controls}). Freezing adaptation at the same moments without correcting the objective does not reproduce this benefit, and the advantage persists at a longer execution budget (\cref{tab:maze-full}).

\subsection{Qualitative analysis of recovery}
\label{sec:qualitative}
In the PushObj example of \cref{fig:teaser}, the native controller with fixed parameters brings the object almost to its goal and then circles within a small neighborhood without closing the remaining pusher error; ECP shares the first fifteen plans, detects the stall, and completes the pusher component two replans later. This pattern is general: across triggered episodes, ECP moves the pusher away from the stalled position within three replans more than three times as often as native planning (\cref{sec:behavior}). In Maze, native AdaJEPA paces the same corridor, whereas stagnation memory makes this corridor costly and leads the agent along a different route. Recorded PushT and Rope rollouts show intermediate observations of further recoveries (\cref{fig:pusht-rollouts,fig:rope-rollouts}).
