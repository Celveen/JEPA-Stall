\begin{abstract}
Latent world models enable action planning, but their controllers can keep moving while repeatedly returning to the same configurations. Existing goal-distance objectives do not use this execution history to avoid repeating unsuccessful behavior. We propose execution-conditioned planning (ECP), which turns observed stagnation into a corrective signal for action selection. ECP stores stalled observations in an episode-local memory and penalizes predicted trajectories that revisit them. Temporary proprioceptive reweighting guides the planner toward unfinished goal components. Conditional activation preserves the original planning behavior before a stall is detected. ECP can be integrated with different world-model backbones without changing their architectures or adding learned parameters, and supports both frozen models and continued online adaptation. We also use interaction-biased data augmentation to enrich offline training with contact-rich transitions. Applied to the state-of-the-art AdaJEPA model, our full approach raises PushObj success to 76.9\%, a gain of 7.4 percentage points. It also achieves 63.7\% success on visually shifted PushT, setting a new state of the art on this benchmark.
\end{abstract}
