\begin{abstract}
World-model planners select actions by minimizing a predicted distance to the goal, but this objective is blind to what the controller has already tried. Latent planners therefore stall: they keep acting while revisiting the same configurations, even when the goal is nearly reached. We introduce Execution-Conditioned Planning (ECP), which turns an episode's own execution history into a corrective planning signal. ECP detects stagnation from realized latent movement, stores stalled observations in an episode-local memory that repels predicted trajectories from revisiting them, and temporarily reweights the goal toward its unfinished proprioceptive component. We pair ECP with interaction-biased data augmentation that supplies contact-rich transitions for learning dynamics. Our training and inference design is inspired by DART's noise-injected data collection and ACT's temporally extended action chunks, respectively. ECP adds no learned parameters, acts entirely through the existing action optimizer, and applies to both frozen and online-adapting world models. On PushObj, our approach raises frozen planning from 43.7\% to 72.3\% and AdaJEPA from 69.4\% to 77.7\%, and the gains hold across a fourfold range of the trigger threshold. On PushT, it attains 86\% clean success and 63.7\% mean success across six visual shifts, the best among all compared world-model controllers, and it improves Maze navigation without a single regression.
\end{abstract}
