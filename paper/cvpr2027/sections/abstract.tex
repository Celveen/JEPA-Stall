\begin{abstract}
Latent world models can repeatedly revisit the same configurations while their controllers continue to act. We address this failure through complementary changes to training data and inference-time planning. Inspired by DAgger's focus on the distribution of visited states and ACT's temporal organization of actions, we connect interaction-rich training experience with recovery sustained across planning steps. Offline, interaction-biased data augmentation increases contact-rich transitions for learning predictive dynamics. Online, execution-conditioned planning (ECP) stores stalled observations in an episode-local memory and penalizes predicted trajectories that revisit them. Temporary proprioceptive reweighting emphasizes unfinished goal components over several planning calls. These corrections support frozen models and continued online adaptation without adding learned parameters. On the inspected PushObj development tasks, augmented AdaJEPA with a tuned trigger reaches 77.7\% success, an 8.3-point gain over the released-model baseline. A geometry-assisted contact-objective extension raises frozen LeWM from 61.4\% to 66.0\% on matched tasks. With the original trigger setting, adaptive ECP achieves 63.7\% success across six PushT visual shifts, the highest mean among the compared controllers.
\end{abstract}
