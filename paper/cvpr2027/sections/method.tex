\section{Execution-conditioned planning}
\label{sec:method}
Our approach couples offline recovery-aware data collection with online closed-loop objective conditioning (\cref{fig:method}). Offline, interaction-biased noisy exploration enriches the experience from which dynamics are learned. Online, ECP monitors execution and, once it detects a stall, modifies subsequent planning objectives via a stagnation memory and a temporary goal-subspace reweighting that remain active across successive planning calls.

\subsection{Latent planning and the ECP objective}
At planning step $t$, the agent receives an observation $o_t=(x_t,p_t)$, consisting of an image $x_t$ and proprioception $p_t$, together with a goal observation $o_g$. An encoder $E_\theta$ maps them to latent states $z_t=(z_t^v,z_t^p)$ and $z_g$, whose visual and proprioceptive branches have dimensions $d_v$ and $d_p$. Given candidate action blocks $A=(a_1,\ldots,a_H)$ over horizon $H$, an action-conditioned predictor $P_\theta$ rolls out future states $\hat z_{t,h}(A)$, $h=1,\ldots,H$. Model predictive control optimizes $A$, executes its first block, and replans from the resulting observation.

Planning quality is measured by a branch-normalized distance balancing visual and proprioceptive alignment:
\begin{equation}
 D_\alpha(z,z')=
 \frac{\norm{z^v-z'^v}^2}{d_v}
 +\alpha\frac{\norm{z^p-z'^p}^2}{d_p},
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
where $\norm{\cdot}$ is the Euclidean norm and $\alpha\geq0$ weights proprioception. The planner's \emph{native} objective $J_{\rm goal}(A;\alpha)$ aggregates this distance to the goal at the terminal frame or across all horizon frames depending on the underlying planner. The native goal term has no explicit memory of failed execution: if execution fails to make progress ($z_{t+1}\approx z_t$), the objective has no explicit record of failed attempts, and replanning can repeat them even as the model adapts. ECP addresses this closed-loop failure by conditioning the objective on the episode's execution history:
\begin{equation}
 A_t^*=\argmin_A\left\{
 J_{\rm goal}(A;\alpha_t)
 +\kappa g_t\mathcal R_t^{\rm stall}(A)\right\}.
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal R_t^{\rm stall}$ is a stagnation regularizer built from an episode memory $\mathcal M_t$, $\kappa$ sets its strength, $g_t\in\{0,1\}$ gates its activation, and $\alpha_t$ is a subspace weight that can be raised temporarily above its base value $\alpha_0$. Until a stall is detected, $\mathcal M_t=\emptyset$ and $\alpha_t=\alpha_0$, so \cref{eq:objective} coincides identically with native planning. ECP modifies only the action objective: the encoder, predictor, and optimizer are untouched, and $\theta$ may stay fixed or be updated online.

\subsection{Detecting stagnation from execution}
\label{sec:trigger}
A stall detector must separate lack of progress from lack of instantaneous motion, since a stalled controller can execute large oscillatory actions and still end where it began. We therefore compare the \emph{net} latent displacement over a window of $W$ observation-feedback steps with the current latent distance to the specified goal:
\begin{equation}
 r_t=\sqrt{\frac{D_{\alpha_0}(z_t,z_{t-W})}
 {\max\{D_{\alpha_0}(z_t,z_g),\epsilon\}}},
 \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
where $z_{t-W}$ is the state $W$ feedback steps earlier and $\epsilon$ is a numerical floor. This dimensionless ratio is small when net motion is small relative to remaining goal distance. It is invariant to uniform latent rescaling when the numerical floor is inactive. We map it to a continuous stall weight,
\begin{equation}
 w_t=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(
 \frac{\gamma_{\rm hi}-r_t}{\gamma_{\rm hi}-\gamma_{\rm lo}}\right),
 \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
which equals one below $\gamma_{\rm lo}$, decreases linearly between the thresholds, and vanishes above $\gamma_{\rm hi}$. Whenever $w_t>0$, the pair $(o_t,w_t)$ is appended to the episode memory $\mathcal M_t$. Because current, past, and goal observations are all re-encoded by the current encoder $E_\theta$, the detector uses a consistent representation during online adaptation.

\subsection{Stagnation memory}
\label{sec:memory}
The memory turns detected stalls into a repulsive potential that discourages predicted trajectories from re-entering stalled neighborhoods while leaving the goal and unrelated regions unaffected. Before each planning call, every stored observation is re-encoded as a memory state $m_j$ ($j\in\mathcal M_t$) with recorded weight $w_j$. Let $d_j=D_{\alpha_0}(m_j,z_g)$ be its squared goal distance. We scale the amplitude $s_j$ and squared support radius $\rho_j$ as follows:
\begin{equation}
 s_j=\frac{d_j}{H+1},\qquad
 \rho_j=\max\{\beta d_j,\epsilon\}.
 \label{eq:memoryscale}
\end{equation}
Here $\beta\in(0,1)$ sets the neighborhood size, and $1/(H+1)$ matches the all-frame goal objective's scale. A compact-support quadratic hinge penalizes predicted states near $m_j$:
\begin{equation}
 K_j(z)=\left[1-\frac{D_{\alpha_0}(z,m_j)}{\rho_j}\right]_+^2,
 \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
where $[u]_+=\max(u,0)$. The kernel equals one at $m_j$ and zero when $D_{\alpha_0}(z,m_j)\geq\rho_j$. Summing these penalties over stored memories and predicted horizon frames gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_t^{\rm stall}(A)
 =\sum_{h=1}^{H}\pi_h
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal M_t}w_js_jK_j(\hat z_{t,h}(A)),
 \label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
where normalized weights $\pi_h=2^h/\sum_{k=1}^{H}2^k$ emphasize later frames to favor trajectories that exit the stalled basin. Memories persist throughout the episode. The fixed-strength Rope variant uses unit memory weights rather than the severity-dependent values $w_j$.

Tying $s_j$ and $\rho_j$ to the goal distance endows $\mathcal R_t^{\rm stall}$ with two theoretical guarantees (proven in \cref{sec:properties}). First, \emph{strict goal exclusion}: because $\beta<1$, the support of each kernel is strictly smaller than its distance to the goal above the floor $\epsilon$, guaranteeing $K_j(z_g)=0$ and $\nabla_z K_j(z_g)=0$ so repulsive basins never block the target. Second, \emph{$C^1$ local repulsion}: both the penalty and its first derivative vanish at and beyond the support boundary, so plans outside stalled neighborhoods are optimized exactly as before.

\subsection{Temporary goal reweighting}
\label{sec:weighting}
Memory tells the planner \emph{where not to return}, but not \emph{what to finish}. A manipulation controller can stall with one goal subspace nearly satisfied and another still open: in PushObj, once the object is nearly aligned ($z_t^v\approx z_g^v$), the remaining error lies in the pusher position ($z_t^p\neq z_g^p$), which the proprioceptive branch of \cref{eq:metric} measures directly. When a stall is detected and $g_t=1$, ECP raises the proprioceptive weight to $\alpha_t=b\,\alpha_0$ ($b>1$) for three consecutive planning calls (renewed by new triggers, and reverting to $\alpha_0$ otherwise). Because the boost is conditional and temporary, the native objective governs nominal progress while subspace amplification completes stalled residuals.

\subsection{Coordinating correction with online adaptation}
\label{sec:gate}
In trigger-only mode, ECP sets $g_t=1$ throughout, and $\mathcal M_t=\emptyset$ guarantees native behavior before the first trigger. When the world model adapts online, however, an early stall may resolve through continued learning. An optional loss gate delays correction until prediction-loss improvement plateaus. Let $\ell_t$ be the mean squared one-step prediction error on the newest executed transition before its weight update, and $\bar\ell_t$ its short running mean. The relative improvement over $W_g$ steps is
\begin{equation}
 q_t=\frac{\bar\ell_{t-W_g}-\bar\ell_t}
 {\max\{\bar\ell_{t-W_g},\epsilon\}}.
 \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
Positive $q_t$ denotes falling loss; zero or negative values indicate a plateau or increase. Starting from $g_t=0$, the gate opens permanently ($g_t=1$) at the first step where the controller is stalled ($w_t>0$) and relative improvement falls below the minimum $\tau_g$ ($q_t<\tau_g$). Observations stalled while $g_t=0$ are logged in $\mathcal M_t$, so recorded stalls are available from the first corrective planning call.

\subsection{Interaction-biased data augmentation}
\label{sec:data}
While ECP corrects stalls online, the training data determines whether the world model has seen recovery interactions in the first place. Following AdaJEPA's contact-biased collection~\cite{wang2026adajepa} and inspired by DART's noise injection~\cite{laskey2017dart}, we add an object-directed bias and Gaussian noise to each source action $u_i$ at step $i$, yielding $u_i+b_c\operatorname{unit}(c_i-p_i)+\sigma_c\eta_i$, where $\operatorname{unit}(\cdot)$ normalizes a nonzero vector, $c_i,p_i\in\R^2$ are the object center and pusher position, $\eta_i\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2)$, and $b_c,\sigma_c$ set the bias and noise scales. Perturbed trajectories containing contact are retained, while the Temporal Straightening training objective~\cite{wang2026straightening} is unchanged (\cref{sec:implementation}).
